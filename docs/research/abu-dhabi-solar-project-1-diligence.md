# Abu Dhabi Solar Opportunity — Project 1 Diligence Pack

**Prepared:** 2026-10-11  
**Status:** Initial diligence framework; no project facts have been independently verified.  
**Scope:** The Abu Dhabi solar opportunity described as land owned by Sheikh Khalifa with a solar-development permit allegedly in place.

## Executive position

Do not present this yet as a bankable solar project. Present it as a **potential site-led solar opportunity subject to verification**.

Before approaching serious investors, IPP developers, EPC contractors, module/inverter suppliers, or technology partners, obtain documentary evidence for five hard gates:

1. **Land and authority:** exact parcel, legal owner, development right, permitted use, term, access, and restrictions.
2. **Solar project scope:** AC export capacity, DC capacity, technology concept, phasing, and intended use of electricity.
3. **Grid/offtake:** point of connection, available export capacity, connection-study status, offtaker, tariff/PPA route, and curtailment risk.
4. **Permitting:** identify exactly what “solar-development permit” means, its issuing authority, validity, conditions, transferability, and remaining approvals.
5. **Commercial control:** who can grant access, sign an exclusivity/term sheet, commit the land, and negotiate the JV or development mandate.

An investor will treat an unverified landowner statement, permit statement, or grid statement as a lead—not as project security.

## Abu Dhabi-specific regulatory observations

- The Abu Dhabi Department of Energy (DoE) regulates and licenses electricity generation and renewable-energy operations. Its licensing framework evaluates technical, managerial, financial, legal, safety, and environmental capability. The DoE also maintains a public register of licences, consents, derogations, and permits. [DoE licensing](https://www.doe.gov.ae/Legislation-and-Compliance/Licensing)
- For a self-supply electricity-generation licence above 5 MW, DoE lists, among other items, a project roadmap, compliance with the Generation Electrical Transmission Code and Electrical Distribution Code, a connection agreement with the transmission/distribution system operator, technical data and performance criteria, a single-line diagram, proposed operator and contractor details, parent-company audited accounts, certificate of completion, and certificates of conformity. This is a useful minimum evidence checklist even if the eventual project is not self-supply. [DoE self-supply licence service](https://www.doe.gov.ae/Services/Issue-New-Electricity-Generation-Self-Supply-Licence)
- DoE states that the licensing regime covers generation, transmission, distribution, supply and storage, as well as renewable energy. Therefore, a land-use or development permit should not automatically be described as an electricity-generation licence or a grid-connection approval. [DoE licensing](https://www.doe.gov.ae/Legislation-and-Compliance/Licensing)
- EWEC describes itself as the sole procurer and supplier of water and electricity in Abu Dhabi and is responsible for planning, forecasting, purchasing, and system dispatch. The project’s intended route to market must therefore be clarified: EWEC-led IPP/PPA, private/off-site offtake, self-supply, behind-the-meter use, or another approved structure. [EWEC planning](https://ewec.ae/Planning)
- Abu Dhabi’s utility-scale solar precedent is structured around large IPP projects connected to the transmission system and supported by a power-purchase arrangement. Noor Abu Dhabi’s official project materials describe a 1,177 MWdc PV plant, a strategic location near the high-voltage transmission grid, and a PPA. This does not prove that the subject site has comparable access; it shows the level of grid and offtake specificity investors will expect. [Noor Abu Dhabi project/green-bond framework](https://www.ewec.ae/public/uploads/mediakit/0.77861900%201718970514/Noor%20Abu%20Dhabi%20Green%20Bond%20Framework.pdf)
- Land-use and development approval must be checked against the applicable Abu Dhabi planning and building framework, not only a verbal statement that a permit exists. DMT’s Binaa library is the relevant official planning/building reference point, but the exact applicable instrument depends on the parcel and municipality. [DMT Binaa](https://binaa.dmt.gov.ae/smart-library/26)
- Environmental baseline work, biodiversity constraints, EAD requirements, and any protected-area restrictions should be screened early. The applicable route depends on the site, its surroundings, and the authority/landowner context; an ADNOC concession, for example, may follow a different internal HSE route. Do not assume one project’s exemption or process applies to this site.

## What the project must define

### 1. Land and site control

Request:

- Exact site name, emirate/municipality/region, GPS coordinates, cadastral or plot number, and a GIS/KMZ boundary.
- Total gross area and net usable area after setbacks, easements, flood channels, roads, pipelines, military/security zones, heritage constraints, and environmental buffers.
- Certified title deed or official land-allocation/lease/usufruct document showing the legal owner and the person/entity authorised to grant development rights.
- Land tenure: ownership, lease, usufruct, concession, allocation, or other right; term; renewal; assignment; mortgage/encumbrance; and termination rights.
- Written landowner consent to develop a solar PV project and to permit surveys, grid studies, environmental studies, construction, financing security, and operation.
- Access rights for construction and operations, including road width, heavy-haul route, bridge limits, turning radii, and any third-party access.
- Existing utilities, pipelines, cables, rights-of-way, wells, contamination, unexploded-ordnance risk, and competing land claims.
- Whether the site is available exclusively for this project and whether any broker, developer, government, or prior investor has rights or claims.

Investor/provider questions:

- Can the project company obtain a bankable long-term land right directly from the owner?
- Can the land right be assigned to lenders or a project company?
- Can the project company mortgage or pledge the project rights and receive step-in protection?
- Is the land large enough after constraints for the proposed capacity, substation, battery, O&M, drainage, and construction laydown?

### 2. Permit and development status

Ask Dominik to send the actual permit, not only its title or number. Capture:

- Issuing authority, permit number, issue date, expiry date, applicant, beneficiary, and current status.
- Exact permitted activity: solar PV generation, self-supply, utility-scale generation, land use, construction, preliminary approval, feasibility, or something else.
- Permitted AC/DC capacity, technology, site boundary, export/import rights, storage rights, and phasing.
- Conditions precedent, milestones, construction deadline, reporting duties, local-content conditions, and revocation triggers.
- Transferability to a new project company, investor, operator, or technology provider.
- Whether the permit is current, suspended, amended, appealed, or dependent on another consent.
- Remaining approvals: DoE generation/development licence, connection approval, environmental approval, construction/building permit, civil-defence/fire approvals, aviation/height/glare approvals, road/access approvals, telecoms, and any municipality or special-zone consent.

The phrase “solar-development permit” should not be used externally until this classification is confirmed.

### 3. Solar resource and technical basis

Request or commission:

- At least one full year of on-site solar irradiation data where possible, plus a bankable long-term satellite dataset and a loss-adjusted P50/P90 energy assessment.
- Preliminary PVsyst or equivalent model showing DC/AC ratio, tilt/azimuth or tracker configuration, clipping, temperature losses, soiling, degradation, availability, mismatch, inverter losses, curtailment, and auxiliary consumption.
- Proposed module technology, efficiency, bifaciality, degradation warranty, certification, manufacturing location, and supply-chain availability.
- Tracker or fixed-tilt concept, wind design basis, pile/foundation assumptions, geotechnical requirements, corrosion conditions, and flood/drainage design.
- Inverter architecture, medium-voltage collection, step-up transformers, substation, protection, SCADA, metering, communications, and grid-code compliance concept.
- Preliminary layout showing DC field, access roads, drainage, substation, transmission line, O&M building, spares, security, fire protection, and construction laydown.
- Water/cleaning strategy: cleaning method, annual water demand if wet cleaning is proposed, water source, quality, wastewater/runoff controls, and whether dry/robotic cleaning is technically and commercially acceptable.
- Heat, dust, humidity, salt-mist, sand abrasion, and extreme-wind design assumptions.

Technology-provider questions:

- What is the guaranteed output and availability, measured at which metering point and under which irradiation/temperature conditions?
- What is the performance-ratio guarantee, annual degradation guarantee, response time, liquidated damages, and warranty backstop?
- Who bears failures caused by soiling, extreme heat, sand abrasion, PID, LID/LeTID, tracker wind stow, flooding, or grid trips?
- Are the components financeable and independently tested? Are warranties assignable to lenders and a buyer?
- Is the provider supplying equipment only, EPC, turnkey EPC, O&M, asset management, or a co-investment/JV role?

### 4. Grid connection and electricity sale

This is usually the decisive gate.

Request:

- Named transmission/distribution system operator and the exact proposed point of connection.
- Voltage level, distance to the connection point, route, substation/bay availability, line capacity, required reinforcement, and preliminary cost estimate.
- Formal grid-connection application, feasibility study, impact study, offer, connection agreement, or written utility correspondence.
- Export capacity in MW AC, import/auxiliary supply requirements, reactive-power requirements, fault-level limits, ramp-rate requirements, voltage/frequency ride-through, protection, islanding, SCADA, dispatch, and metering requirements.
- Construction and energisation milestones, security deposits, connection charges, reinforcement responsibility, and long-stop dates.
- Curtailment regime, deemed energy, change-in-law treatment, grid unavailability, force majeure, and who pays for network upgrades.
- Offtake route: EWEC PPA, private corporate PPA, sleeved PPA, self-supply, behind-the-meter, merchant sale, clean-energy certificate value, or hybrid.
- Buyer identity, credit quality, tenor, contracted energy profile, price/indexation, currency, payment security, termination compensation, and change-of-control provisions.

Critical distinction: a nearby high-voltage line is not the same as available connection capacity. Only a written utility/grid assessment can support the claim.

### 5. Commercial and financing case

Prepare a preliminary model with:

- Total installed cost split into modules, inverters, trackers, civil works, electrical balance of plant, substation, transmission line, development, land, permitting, owner’s costs, financing fees, contingency, and taxes/duties.
- Annual operating costs: O&M, land rent, insurance, security, cleaning, water, spare parts, asset management, grid charges, licence/compliance, and decommissioning reserve.
- Energy case: P50/P90 production, degradation, availability, curtailment, auxiliary consumption, and net delivered energy.
- Revenue case: PPA/offtake price, escalation/indexation, certificates, capacity or ancillary value if applicable, and downside cases.
- Financing assumptions: debt/equity ratio, tenor, interest, DSCR, reserve accounts, guarantees, sponsor support, hedging, and lender technical-adviser costs.
- Sensitivities: lower irradiation, higher soiling, higher capex, delayed COD, lower tariff, curtailment, grid reinforcement cost, FX exposure, module replacement, and interest-rate movement.
- Development-stage budget and who funds it before financial close.

Investor questions:

- Who is the sponsor and what capital, development track record, balance sheet, and guarantees do they bring?
- Is there a credible offtaker and a bankable PPA before construction financing is sought?
- Is the proposed return based on a contracted infrastructure asset or on speculative merchant electricity sales?
- What rights does the investor receive: equity, preferred return, development fee, carried interest, exclusivity, board control, or exit rights?
- What happens if the permit, grid offer, land right, or PPA is not obtained?
- Who owns the studies and development rights if the project does not proceed?

### 6. Environmental, social, HSE, and end-of-life

Confirm:

- Required environmental assessment and authority; protected areas, habitats, groundwater, biodiversity, and wildlife impacts.
- Glare and glint assessment, aviation constraints, radar/telecommunications interference, visual impact, dust, noise, and construction traffic.
- Soil, groundwater, flood, drainage, contamination, archaeological, and unexploded-ordnance surveys as applicable.
- Construction HSE plan, emergency response, fire protection, electrical safety, worker accommodation/transport, and contractor standards.
- Module, inverter, battery (if any), transformer-oil, and waste-management obligations.
- Decommissioning, land restoration, salvage, recycling, and financial security requirements.

### 7. Parties, authority, and transaction structure

Before introductions, map:

- Landowner.
- Permit holder/applicant.
- Project sponsor/developer.
- Proposed project company.
- Offtaker.
- Grid/transmission/distribution operator.
- Government/municipal authorities.
- EPC contractor.
- Module, inverter, tracker, storage, and SCADA providers.
- O&M and asset manager.
- Equity investor and lenders.

For each party record legal name, role, authority, evidence, contact, and whether the role is proposed or already contracted. Avoid describing a company as “operator,” “investor,” “offtaker,” or “partner” merely because it has been contacted.

## Questions to send Dominik before spending time on introductions

### Minimum first request

1. What is the exact site location, plot number, GPS boundary, gross area, and usable area?
2. Who is the registered landowner, and what document proves ownership or the right to grant the project?
3. What exactly is the “solar-development permit”? Please send the full permit, all annexes, and any amendment or renewal.
4. Which authority issued it, for whom, for what capacity, for what technology, and until what date?
5. Is the permit transferable to a project company or investor? If so, what approval is required?
6. What is the proposed plant size in MW AC and MW DC? Is battery storage included or excluded?
7. Is this intended as a utility-scale IPP, self-supply, behind-the-meter project, private PPA, or another model?
8. Who is the intended electricity buyer/offtaker? Is there a signed letter of intent, PPA, tariff indication, or only an assumption?
9. Has a grid-connection application or study been submitted? Please provide the utility correspondence, point of connection, voltage, available capacity, upgrade scope, cost, and timeline.
10. Which approvals remain outstanding, including DoE, construction, environmental, municipality, civil defence, aviation/glare, access, and telecoms approvals?
11. Is the land free of mortgages, easements, competing claims, protected-area constraints, pipelines, cables, or other development restrictions?
12. Who has authority to sign an NDA, site-access agreement, exclusivity, term sheet, land agreement, JV agreement, or mandate?
13. What is Dominik’s proposed commercial structure: land lease, JV, development fee, equity contribution, success fee, or sale of a development right?
14. What investment amount is expected, and what has already been spent on studies, permits, grid work, and development?
15. What work has already been completed: topographical, geotechnical, solar-resource, environmental, drainage, glare, grid, and financial studies?
16. Are there any existing commitments, exclusivity arrangements, brokers, prior investors, or competing developers?
17. What decision and response timetable is expected from us, and what access will be provided for technical and legal diligence?

### Documents to request in the same message

- Land title/allocation/lease/usufruct and site plan.
- Full permit and annexes.
- Official land-use/planning confirmation.
- Grid application, study, offer, or connection agreement.
- Any PPA, offtake letter, tariff indication, or buyer correspondence.
- Existing technical, solar-resource, geotechnical, environmental, and feasibility studies.
- Corporate documents and signatory authority for the landowner/permit holder/project sponsor.
- Any prior term sheet, mandate, broker agreement, exclusivity, or investor correspondence relevant to the site.

## How to classify Dominik’s answers

Use three statuses:

- **Verified:** supported by an official authority document, signed utility/operator confirmation, signed land document, or independently checkable corporate record.
- **Unverified lead:** stated by Dominik or another party but not supported by a primary document.
- **Contradicted/unclear:** inconsistent documents, expired permit, unclear authority, or a claim that cannot be reconciled.

Do not circulate an investor teaser with hard claims such as “land owned by Sheikh Khalifa,” “permit in place,” “grid available,” or “ready to build” until the supporting documents are reviewed.

## Recommended execution sequence

1. Obtain the minimum first-request documents from Dominik.
2. Run a document and authority check: land, permit, capacity, validity, transferability, and signatory authority.
3. Commission a desktop technical screen: usable area, indicative DC/AC sizing, solar resource, access, grid distance, environmental red flags, and capex range.
4. Confirm the offtake route and grid pathway before soliciting detailed technology bids.
5. Issue a controlled, non-binding RFQ to a short list of operators/EPC/technology providers with the same data room and assumptions.
6. Only after a credible technical/offtake case exists, approach investors with a data-room index, risk register, model, development budget, and proposed transaction structure.

## Current red flags

- No exact site, boundary, land document, permit copy, capacity, grid point, or offtaker has yet been provided.
- “Permit already in place” may refer to planning/development permission rather than electricity-generation licensing.
- A solar operator cannot price or commit responsibly without site, grid, offtake, and authority information.
- A technology provider may provide a preliminary concept, but a bankable guarantee requires defined metering, irradiance, degradation, availability, grid, and interface conditions.
- A serious investor will likely decline or remain non-committal if land control, grid access, and revenue are still verbal.
